## The tiers

| Tier | Structure | Frames | Rollback | For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cold | Archetype chunks, AoS | 0 | No | Rarely touched, not iterated |
| Static | Read-only array | 0 | No | Geometry that never changes |
| Volatile | SoA ring | 3 | No | Cosmetic entities, ambient AI, particles |
| Temporal | SoA ring | max(8, N) | Yes | Networked, simulation-authoritative |

{/* TODO: the Cold row is the point and it should be said out loud. Plain AoS
    archetype chunks, the conventional thing, kept deliberately for data where
    column decomposition would cost more than it returns. A post arguing for
    field-granular columns is more credible when it also says where they lose. */}

## You pay for time travel only where you need it

{/* TODO
      - Rollback capability is a per-component property, not a global mode.
      - When rollback is compiled out, Temporal silently downgrades to
        Volatile, so a build that cannot use history does not allocate it.
      - Ring depth is max(8, configured); the floor of 8 covers the physics
        divisor. */}

{/* MEASUREMENTS - what history costs
    Source: Trinyx docs/reference/Performance-Targets.md. Measured 2026-05,
    RelWithDebInfo. Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX / Ryzen 9 9950X, 32-64GB,
    RTX 5070 Ti / RTX 4070, CachyOS and Windows 11. Tracy for frame timing.

    Temporal slab at 100K entities:
      8-frame ring (minimum)          ~270MB
      field data at rollback check    ~34MB
    Per-frame SaveSnapshot overhead   <0.1ms
    History Write budget              0.2ms of a 1.95ms frame
*/}
