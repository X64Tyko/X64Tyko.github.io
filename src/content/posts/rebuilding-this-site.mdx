This site started as a scratch repo for learning React and TypeScript. It was
the Vite starter template with a tic-tac-toe board bolted onto the counter
button. Once I felt comfortable with React and moved on, it sat unused.
This update aims to remedy that. I have a lot of thoughts and opinions across
a variety of subjects. While this is a common sentiment among blogs, I do
believe my particular experience gives me some authority on the areas
addressed: programming, gaming, and design, both as a gamer and developer.

There's a practical reason too. I love making things, but there's simply not
enough time in the day. Building anything real needs a long uninterrupted run,
and most of my time shows up in pieces too small to even reload where I was,
at least while keeping my sleep schedule intact.

Writing fits in those pieces. A paragraph is something I can actually finish. I'm
hoping this is something I can keep up with more consistently than the fractured
development time I actually get.

It also pins down what I thought at the time. Opinions change quietly. You shift,
your memory shifts with you, and you end up sure you always thought the current
thing. There's nothing to learn from that.

Writing it down stops the quiet part. It's version control for opinions, and I
can diff it against myself later.

That's part of why I kept the tic-tac-toe board. The downside is obvious: I'll be
wrong in public, with a date on it, and it's all indexed. I think that's fine. If
I'm not willing to write an opinion down, it's already a target for more internal
critique before bringing it to public debate.

## What it is now

An [Astro](https://astro.build) site. Posts are MDX files in
`src/content/posts/`, validated against a schema at build time, so a malformed
date or a missing title fails the build instead of rendering an empty page.

The parts that mattered when picking it:

- **Static output.** A prose page ships as HTML. No framework runtime loads
  unless a page actually needs one.
- **MDX with components.** Posts are Markdown, but a post that needs an
  interactive diagram can import a React component and render it inline.
- **Feeds and metadata are built in.** RSS, sitemap, and per-page Open Graph
  tags come from the content schema rather than being maintained by hand.

## What the React detour was worth

I kept the tic-tac-toe board. Nothing on the site links to it any more, but it's
still in the repo, unchanged, missing `key` props and win detection and all.

I left it because one line of it turned out to be the most interesting thing I
got out of learning React. Changing a single cell of a nine-cell board means
allocating a new array of nine, and working out *why* that's the correct way to
write it is what convinced me that immutability in web frameworks is an
optimization wearing the costume of a moral principle. That
argument got long enough to need [its own
post](/blog/immutability-is-not-a-virtue/).

## What I'm planning to write about

Three rough areas, and the split will probably move.

**Engineering.** Engine architecture and data-oriented design, mostly out of
building my own engine. Two threads so far. Code Critical is hard looks at
patterns that get repeated without their reasons, which is where the
immutability post lives. Design Dissection is the other register: how a thing
actually works and what it costs. Importantly, with the context to determine
when the design is valuable or not.

**Games.** The same dissection pointed at design instead of code. Rail shooters
first, because the fixed camera keeps getting written about as a limitation when
it's the entire point. Reviews too, read for their systems rather than for a
score.

**Industry.** Critique and analysis of the games industry, which is in enough of
a state right now to deserve some. Decisions that look opaque from the outside
are usually made under constraints nobody outside can see, and I'd rather write
about those than hand out blame. A window into what actually goes on, and into
the thinking of the people doing it at every level of it.

That's the current plan and it's still churning. Some of this gets written, some
gets quietly dropped. Going back later to see which is half the point.
